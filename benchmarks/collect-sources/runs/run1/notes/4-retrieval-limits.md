# What are the retrieval-side and architecture-side limits of RAG for hallucination?

## In brief
- Retrieval failures are a first-order cause of RAG hallucination: RAG "relies heavily on the relevance of retrieved documents" [crag], and irrelevant content passed downstream "can lead to hallucinations" [finetune-rag].
- Dense-retriever recall failures surface as hard negatives: short chunked units "may increase the likelihood of introducing hard negatives during the retrieval stage" [longrag]; systems "suffer from limitations inherent to information retrieval systems" [sevenfailures].
- Corrupt/unverifiable sources: static corpora "can only return sub-optimal documents" [crag]; fine-tuning against a dataset mimicking "real-world imperfections" recovers 21.2% factual accuracy [finetune-rag].
- Chunking has no universal optimum: 64–128 tokens best for fact-based answers, 512–1024 for context-heavy ones, and sensitivity depends on the embedding model [chunksize]; ~4K-token units cut hard negatives on NQ/HotpotQA [longrag].
- Self-reflective/iterative additions: FLARE retrieves mid-generation using next-sentence predictions gated by low-confidence tokens [flare]; Self-RAG learns on-demand retrieval plus reflection tokens, beating RAG-Llama2-chat on QA/fact verification [selfrag]; CRAG adds a retrieval evaluator and web-search fallback [crag].
- Long context beats RAG "when resourced sufficiently" but costs far more [li2024lc], and suffers position bias: performance degrades for information "in the middle of long contexts, even for explicitly long-context models" [lost-in-middle].

## Findings
1. Misretrieval as hallucination driver. CRAG (Yan et al., 2024, arXiv 2401.15884): "Although retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a practicable complement to LLMs, it relies heavily on the relevance of retrieved documents, raising concerns about how the model behaves if retrieval goes wrong." [crag] Why: frames misretrieval as a robustness problem, not just an accuracy problem.
2. Finetune-RAG (Lee et al., 2025, arXiv 2505.10792): "ensuring perfect retrieval of relevant information remains challenging, and when irrelevant content is passed downstream to an LLM, it can lead to hallucinations." [finetune-rag] Why: direct empirical claim that imperfect retrieval causes hallucination; their fine-tune "improves factual accuracy by 21.2% over the base model", and Bench-RAG "stress tests models under realistic imperfect retrieval scenarios."
3. Seven Failure Points (Barnett et al., CAIN 2024, doi 10.1145/3644815.3644945): "RAG systems suffer from limitations inherent to information retrieval systems and from reliance on LLMs"; "validation of a RAG system is only feasible during operation." [sevenfailures] Why: practitioner corroboration from three case studies (research, education, biomedical).
4. Hard negatives from retrieval. LongRAG (Jiang et al., 2024, arXiv 2406.15319): "The loss of contextual information in the short, chunked units may increase the likelihood of introducing hard negatives during the retrieval stage." [longrag] Why: identifies a "heavy" retriever / "light" reader imbalance; 4K-token units cut 22M→600K units, need "less than 8" top units, and reach "EM of 62.7% on NQ and 64.3% on HotpotQA, which are on par with the (fully-trained) SoTA model."
5. Corrupt/unverifiable sources. CRAG: "Since retrieval from static and limited corpora can only return sub-optimal documents, large-scale web searches are utilized as an extension"; a "decompose-then-recompose algorithm... selectively focus[es] on key information and filter[s] out irrelevant information." [crag] Why: the standard corrective answer to unreliable corpora; plug-and-play, tested on four short-/long-form datasets.
6. Chunk granularity. Rethinking Chunk Size (Bhat et al., 2025, arXiv 2505.21700): "smaller chunks (64-128 tokens) are optimal for datasets with concise, fact-based answers, whereas larger chunks (512-1024 tokens) improve retrieval in datasets requiring broader contextual understanding." [chunksize] Why: granularity interacts with the embedding model — "models like Stella benefit from larger chunks... Snowflake performs better with smaller chunks."
7. FLARE (Jiang et al., EMNLP 2023, arXiv 2305.06983): "iteratively uses a prediction of the upcoming sentence to anticipate future content, which is then utilized as a query to retrieve relevant documents to regenerate the sentence if it contains low-confidence tokens." [flare] Why: attacks the single-retrieve limitation — "a retrieve-and-generate setup that only retrieves information once... is limiting" for long texts; tested on 4 long-form knowledge-intensive tasks.
8. Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2023, arXiv 2310.11511; ICLR 2024): "trains a single arbitrary LM that adaptively retrieves passages on-demand, and generates and reflects on retrieved passages and its own generations using special tokens, called reflection tokens." [selfrag] Why: motivates that "indiscriminately retrieving... a fixed number of retrieved passages... can lead to unhelpful response generation"; 7B/13B "outperforms ChatGPT and retrieval-augmented Llama2-chat on Open-domain QA, reasoning and fact verification tasks."
9. CRAG's gate: "a lightweight retrieval evaluator is designed to assess the overall quality of retrieved documents for a query, returning a confidence degree based on which different knowledge retrieval actions can be triggered." [crag] Why: all three corrective methods gate on a model-scored confidence signal — the correction is only as good as that signal.
10. Long context. RAG vs LC (Li et al., EMNLP 2024 Industry, arXiv 2407.16833): "when resourced sufficiently, LC consistently outperforms RAG in terms of average performance. However, RAG's significantly lower cost remains a distinct advantage." [li2024lc] Their Self-Route routes each query to RAG or LC via self-reflection. Lost in the Middle (Liu et al., TACL 2024, arXiv 2307.03172): "performance is often highest when relevant information occurs at the beginning or end of the input context, and significantly degrades when models must access relevant information in the middle of long contexts, even for explicitly long-context models." [lost-in-middle] Why: U-shaped primacy/recency bias is a hard limit for LC approaches and for any RAG that packs many passages.
11. Framing: the Naive/Advanced/Modular taxonomy (Gao et al., survey, arXiv 2312.10997) places each fix at a different stage: "a detailed examination of the progression of RAG paradigms, encompassing the Naive RAG, the Advanced RAG, and the Modular RAG." [gao-survey] Why: organizes where retrieval-side vs architecture-side limits live.
- Unresolved after all of the above (synthesis): retrieval quality still bottlenecks corrected/iterative designs [crag][finetune-rag][sevenfailures]; LC trades cost for quality and is position-biased [li2024lc][lost-in-middle]; self-reflective gating inherits the model's own imperfect confidence [flare][selfrag][crag]; chunking optimum is dataset/model-dependent [chunksize][longrag].

## Conflicts
- LC vs RAG: [li2024lc] finds "LC consistently outperforms RAG" in average performance, while [longrag] shows long-unit RAG matches fully-trained SoTA on NQ/HotpotQA. Reconcilable: different tasks (multi-document QA vs general) and cost regimes; [li2024lc] itself concedes RAG's cost advantage.
- Chunk size: [chunksize] finds 64–128 tokens optimal for fact-based retrieval, while [longrag] argues for ~30x larger 4K-token units. Different datasets (mixed short/long-form vs Wikipedia NQ/HotpotQA) and embedding models; unresolved.

## Gaps
- No cited source quantifies hallucination from factually *wrong* (corrupt) retrieved documents versus merely irrelevant ones; evidence covers imperfect retrieval in general [finetune-rag]. Corpus-poisoning defenses exist (RAGPart/RAGMask, arXiv 2512.24268) but were not fetched.
- Self-RAG/FLARE/CRAG report overall accuracy/factuality gains but do not isolate hallucination-rate deltas on retrieval-failure subsets; latency/cost of iterative retrieval is only addressed in [li2024lc].
- CRAG, LongRAG, and the chunking studies are non-peer-reviewed preprints (B); CRAG's acceptance status unconfirmed.
- Passage *order* effects on faithfulness (distinct from [lost-in-middle]'s position effects on task performance) are not directly measured in my sources.
