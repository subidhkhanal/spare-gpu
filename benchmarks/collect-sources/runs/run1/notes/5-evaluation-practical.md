# What are the evaluation-side and practical limits of using RAG to reduce hallucination?

## In brief
- Standard RAG evaluation is reference-free faithfulness / answer-relevance / context-relevance (RAGAS, 2023); the practical fix for annotation cost is synthetic judge training data plus a small human-anchored calibration set (ARES, 2024) [ragas2023, ares2024].
- LLM-as-judge metrics carry documented weaknesses: position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases, limited reasoning ability (2023) [zheng2023].
- Even production RAG marketed as "hallucination-free" hallucinated on 17–33% of real legal queries (2024 preprint; JELS 2025) [magesh2025].
- A significant fraction of RAG-generated sentences stay ungrounded even when the final answer is correct (2024) [stolfo2024].
- Practical limits: retrieval adds end-to-end latency (2025, researcher-measured) [ragcache2025]; more retrieved chunks can degrade quality, so context budgets matter (2024) [carrot2024]; evolving corpora break static indexes and miss superseded facts (2026) [nuggetindex2026].
- Open questions (survey, 2024): robustness to noise/contradictions, RAG vs long-context, scaling laws, and "a need to refine evaluation methodologies to keep pace with its evolution" [gaosurvey2024]; static benchmarks are further undermined by data contamination (2023) [sainz2023].

## Findings
Evaluation metrics and their weaknesses
1. Reference-free faithfulness metrics. RAGAS (2023, arXiv:2309.15217): "Evaluating RAG architectures is, however, challenging because there are several dimensions to consider: the ability of the retrieval system to identify relevant and focused context passages, the ability of the LLM to exploit such passages in a faithful way, or the quality of the generation itself"; metrics work "without having to rely on ground truth human annotations" [ragas2023]. Why: defines the dominant metric family; its reference-free design means metric reliability rests on NLI/LLM judgments.
2. LLM-as-judge reliability. Zheng et al. (2023, NeurIPS): "We examine the usage and limitations of LLM-as-a-judge, including position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases, as well as limited reasoning ability, and propose solutions to mitigate some of them" [zheng2023]. Why: RAG faithfulness metrics delegate judging to LLMs; these biases directly threaten them.
3. Synthetic-data gap and mitigation. ARES (NAACL 2024): "Evaluating retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems traditionally relies on hand annotations… By creating its own synthetic training data, ARES finetunes lightweight LM judges… To mitigate potential prediction errors, ARES utilizes a small set of human-annotated datapoints for prediction-powered inference (PPI)" [ares2024]. Why: synthetic judge data is the standard workaround, but its error mode requires human-anchored correction.
4. Groundedness does not follow from RAG. Stolfo (Findings NAACL 2024): "Across 3 datasets and 4 model families, our findings reveal that a significant fraction of generated sentences are consistently ungrounded, even when those sentences contain correct ground-truth answers" [stolfo2024]. Why: ungroundedness persists under RAG; grounding must be measured, not assumed.
5. Production RAG is not hallucination-free. Magesh et al. (arXiv 2024-05-30; JELS 2025): "While hallucinations are reduced relative to general-purpose chatbots (GPT-4), we find that the AI research tools made by LexisNexis (Lexis+ AI) and Thomson Reuters (Westlaw AI-Assisted Research and Ask Practical Law AI) each hallucinate between 17% and 33% of the time" [magesh2025]. Why: strongest real-world evidence that RAG reduces but does not eliminate hallucination; pre-registered evaluation refutes vendor "hallucination-free" claims.
6. Benchmark contamination. Sainz et al. (Findings EMNLP 2023): "The worst kind of data contamination happens when a Large Language Model (LLM) is trained on the test split of a benchmark, and then evaluated in the same benchmark. The extent of the problem is unknown, as it is not straightforward to measure" [sainz2023]. Why: static RAG/hallucination benchmarks may overestimate performance and are hard to decontaminate.

Practical issues
7. Latency. RAGCache (ACM TOCS 2025): "the retrieval step introduces long sequence generation and extra data dependency, resulting in long end-to-end latency" (authors' own benchmark; not vendor-reported) [ragcache2025]. Gao et al. (2024) agree: RAG "comes with higher latency and ethical considerations regarding data retrieval" [gaosurvey2024].
8. Cost of retrieval + larger prompts. CARROT (2024, ICDE 2026): "the utility of chunks is non-monotonic, as adding more chunks can degrade quality"; optimal chunk selection is framed as "cost-constrained" optimization instead of "budget exhaustion" [carrot2024]. Gao et al. (2024): "Larger chunks can capture more context, but they also generate more noise, requiring longer processing time and higher costs" [gaosurvey2024].
9. Index maintenance / staleness / domain drift. NuggetIndex (SIGIR 2026): "This unit mismatch between evaluation and retrieval objects hinders maintenance when corpora evolve and fails to capture superseded facts or source disagreements" [nuggetindex2026]. Gao et al. (2024) treat freshness as an open problem addressable via timestamp-weighted retrieval ("time-aware RAG, ensuring the freshness of knowledge") [gaosurvey2024].
10. Engineering complexity and open questions. Gao et al. (2024): "enhancing retrieval efficiency, improving document recall in large knowledge bases, and ensuring data security—such as preventing inadvertent disclosure of document sources or metadata by LLMs—are critical engineering challenges that remain to be addressed" [gaosurvey2024]. Same survey lists robustness to noisy/contradictory retrieval ("Misinformation can be worse than no information at all"; counterintuitively, "including irrelevant documents can unexpectedly increase accuracy by over 30%"), uncertain scaling laws for RAG, and RAG-vs-long-context design as future trends, closing with "there is a need to refine evaluation methodologies to keep pace with its evolution" [gaosurvey2024].

## Conflicts
- Vendor claims that RAG "eliminates" or is "hallucination-free" vs the preregistered finding of 17–33% hallucination rates; the stronger source (peer-reviewed, preregistered) states "the providers' claims are overstated" [magesh2025].
- ARES (2024) reports its judges "remain effective across domain shifts" [ares2024] while LLM-judge studies document systematic bias and reliability limits (2023) [zheng2023]; no head-to-head comparison found.
- Intuition that more retrieved context improves grounding is contradicted by CARROT's non-monotonic chunk utility (2024) [carrot2024] and the >30% accuracy gain from irrelevant documents reported in Gao (2024) [gaosurvey2024].

## Gaps
- No source within the 10-source cap quantifies a synthetic-data gap specific to RAG evaluation benchmarks (distinct from generic LLM benchmark contamination).
- Entity-level attribution blind spot: faithfulness/citation checks can pass while evidence is attributed to the wrong entity ("deceptive grounding," arXiv:2607.09349, 2026) — identified but dropped due to source cap.
- No vendor-reported latency/cost figures found within cap; all cost/latency numbers kept are academic measurements (RAGCache 2025, CARROT 2024) or qualitative survey statements (Gao 2024).
