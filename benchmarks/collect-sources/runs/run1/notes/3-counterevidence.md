# Sceptic view: evidence that RAG does NOT reliably reduce hallucination (can worsen outputs)

## In brief
- Retrieval can actively hurt: RAG accuracy drops when retrieved passages are irrelevant, with cascading errors in multi-hop settings [yoran2024irrelevant].
- Models are unfaithful to evidence in both directions: under knowledge conflict they "frequently ignore the context and revert to memorized priors" [yu2026fides], yet also suppress correct parametric knowledge when context is irrelevant or merely complementary [cheng2024echoqa].
- Position and order distort context use: U-shaped "lost in the middle" degradation [liu2024lost]; answers vary substantially across document permutations even with the gold document fixed first [zhang2026stablerag].
- Noise and length degrade even 2025 frontier models: performance "consistently degrades" with input length; one distractor already hurts [hong2025contextrot].
- One malicious document in the knowledge base suffices to hijack a production RAG system [chaudhari2024phantom].
- In a head-to-head, long-context LLMs beat RAG on average performance; RAG's edge is cost, not reliability [liu2024raglc].

## Findings
1. **Irrelevant context reduces accuracy.** "However, recent work has shown that retrieval augmentation can sometimes have a negative effect on performance"; in multi-hop reasoning "misuse of irrelevant evidence can lead to cascading errors" [yoran2024irrelevant]. Task/data: five open-domain QA benchmarks, retrieval-augmented seq2seq LMs. Why: earliest systematic proof (2023) that retrieval is a liability, not just a no-op.
2. **Distracting effect is quantifiable and cross-model.** "Retrieved passages that are irrelevant to the query sometimes distract the answer-generating LLM, causing it to provide an incorrect response"; the effect is "robust across LLMs" [amiraz2025distracting]. Task/data: open-domain RAG QA, multiple LLMs (ACL 2025). Why: passage-level distractibility is a first-class failure mode, not an edge case.
3. **Over-reliance on parametric memory / contradicting retrieved evidence.** "When retrieved evidence contradicts parametric memory, language models frequently ignore the context and revert to memorized priors—a failure that undermines the purpose of retrieval augmentation" [yu2026fides]. Task/data: three QA benchmarks, six backbones 7B–70B, NQ-Swap conflict pairs (EMNLP 2026). Why: direct, quantified evidence; even at 70B context fidelity plateaus at 92–94%, i.e., failures persist.
4. **Mirror failure: over-trust of context.** "LLMs tend to suppress their PK when contextual information is available, even when it is complementary or irrelevant" [cheng2024echoqa]. Task/data: ECHOQA benchmark (scientific, factual, commonsense QA), 2024. Why: correct internal knowledge gets discarded when noisy/complementary context is present — RAG outputs can be worse than closed-book.
5. **Lost in the middle.** "Performance is often highest when relevant information occurs at the beginning or end of the input context, and significantly degrades when models must access relevant information in the middle of long contexts, even for explicitly long-context models" [liu2024lost]. Task/data: multi-document QA + key-value retrieval; multi-model (TACL 2024). Why: the canonical dilution result — where a retrieved passage sits changes the answer.
6. **Permutation-induced hallucinations.** "Under a Top-5 retrieval setting with the gold document included, LLM answers vary substantially across permutations of the retrieved set, even when the gold document is fixed in the first position" [zhang2026stablerag]. Task/data: three QA datasets (ACL 2026). Why: correct retrieval + correct top position is still not enough; document order flips answers.
7. **Context rot at scale (2025).** "Across all experiments, model performance consistently degrades with increasing input length"; "Even a single distractor reduces performance relative to the baseline"; on LongMemEval, adding irrelevant context to ~113k-token prompts "significantly impacts a model's ability to maintain reliable performance" [hong2025contextrot]. Task/data: 18 LLMs (GPT-4.1, Claude 4, Gemini 2.5, Qwen3); NIAH extensions, LongMemEval, repeated-words; Chroma report, 2025-07-14. Why: dilution is not solved in current frontier models; B-rated but methodologically controlled (task complexity held constant).
8. **Adversarial retrieved passages.** Phantom "allows an adversary to inject a single malicious document into a RAG system's knowledge base, and mount a backdoor poisoning attack"; demonstrated on "an end-to-end black-box production RAG system: NVIDIA's 'Chat with RTX'" [chaudhari2024phantom]. Task/data: backdoor attacks on RAG; Gemma, Vicuna, Llama; transfers to GPT-3.5/GPT-4 (2024, v3 2025). Why: a single poisoned passage can flip outputs — susceptibility is exploitable, not hypothetical.
9. **Imperfect retrieval is the norm, and harmful.** "We find that imperfect retrieval augmentation is inevitable, common, and harmful"; knowledge conflicts between internal and retrieved knowledge are "a bottleneck" [wang2024astute]. Task/data: controlled RAG analyses under realistic conditions with Gemini and Claude (ACL 2025). Why: frames irrelevant/misleading/malicious retrieval as the default operating condition, not an anomaly.
10. **RAG is not the more reliable option when resources allow.** "Results reveal that when resourced sufficiently, LC consistently outperforms RAG in terms of average performance. However, RAG's significantly lower cost remains a distinct advantage" [liu2024raglc]. Task/data: three latest LLMs (Gemini-1.5, GPT-4 family), various public datasets (EMNLP 2024 industry). Why: RAG's advantage is economic, not epistemic.

## Conflicts
- Direction of the trust bias: ECHOQA finds models over-trust context (suppress parametric knowledge even when context is irrelevant/complementary) [cheng2024echoqa], while FIDES finds models "frequently ignore the context and revert to memorized priors" [yu2026fides]. Both A-rated; the sources I read do not reconcile the two — plausibly both occur, governed by memory strength vs. context plausibility.
- Needle-in-a-Haystack suggests long context is near-solved, but Chroma's controlled sweep (plus NoLiMa, AbsenceBench cited therein) finds consistent degradation [hong2025contextrot]; the more controlled evidence supports degradation.
- RAG advocates treat retrieval as the hallucination fix; liu2024raglc finds LC outperforms RAG on average [liu2024raglc] — no direct head-to-head rebuttal located.

## Gaps
- Abstract-level detail only (standard depth): full model/dataset lists per study (e.g., yoran2024irrelevant's exact five benchmarks) not enumerated in abstracts; would require full PDFs.
- No head-to-head study found that measures RAG vs. closed-book hallucination *rates* directly; most counter-evidence measures accuracy/context fidelity, with a few (Stable-RAG, FIDES) framed in hallucination terms.
- "To Trust or Not to Trust?" (arXiv:2410.14675) — models "tend to overly rely on external information, regardless of its factual accuracy" — noted but dropped for the 10-source cap; would strengthen finding 4.
- Poison-RAG (arXiv:2501.11759) and other 2025 knowledge-poisoning papers found but not kept (source cap); Phantom covers the adversarial angle.
