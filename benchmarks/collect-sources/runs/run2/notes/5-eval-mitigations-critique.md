# SQ5: RAG hallucination measurement, mitigations beyond basic RAG, and counter-evidence

## In brief
(a) Faithfulness/groundedness in RAG is mostly measured with LLM judges (RAGAS-style claim decomposition, Vectara HHEM/FaithJudge leaderboards) and NLI/lexical attribution scorers; recent work finds judge scores unreliable, attribution metrics non-transferable across constructs, and verifier strictness alone swinging reported unsupported-citation rates from ~3% to ~18% [ragas, vectara-faithjudge, ding2026, goo2026].
(b) Mitigations (self-checking, citation verification, abstention, conflict checks, conformal self-consistency) do show empirical gains — Self-RAG style adaptive retrieval, generator conflict checks 41.6%→13.3%, conformal abstention bounds error — but each trades coverage for safety, costs inference, or fails when parametric memory agrees with bad context [selfrag, grab-rag, kuzborskij2024].
(c) Strongest counter-evidence: preregistered tests of flagship commercial RAG legal tools found 17–33% hallucination despite vendor "hallucination-free" claims; RAG suppresses abstention while hallucination stays high (naive 87.6% → advanced 65.4%, abstention 5%→0%); accuracy-when-answering is nearly tied across deployed systems while canary-violation differs ~6×, so volume-based scores reward guessing [magesh2024, reasons2024, penalty2026].

## Findings

### (a) Measurement and its limits
- Claim: reference-free faithfulness is computed by LLMs decomposing answers into claims (RAGAS) — the dominant pattern for RAG eval. Quote: "We introduce Ragas (Retrieval Augmented Generation Assessment), a framework for reference-free evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines." [ragas] Why: defines the canonical metric family the checklist asks about.
- Claim: even with relevant context supplied, models still emit unsupported claims; detection methods themselves were found limited, prompting Vectara's judge redesign. Quote: "yet large language models (LLMs) still frequently introduce unsupported information or contradictions even when provided with relevant context… Motivated by limitations observed in current hallucination detection methods, we introduce FaithJudge, an LLM-as-a-judge framework…" [vectara-faithjudge] Why: documents both (a) persistence of hallucination under RAG and (b) acknowledged flaws of existing detectors (dated 2025-05, rev. 2025-11).
- Claim: faithfulness-metric review finds LLM judges correlate best with humans but the field's metrics remain heterogeneous. Quote: "We find that the use of Large Language Models (LLMs) as a faithfulness evaluator is commonly the metric that is most highly correlated with human judgement." [malin2025] Why: shows why judges dominate despite their weaknesses.
- Claim: attribution scores do not transfer across evaluation constructs — a scorer good for provenance need not work for entailment. Quote: "We audit eight automatic scorers — lexical, embedding, and BERTScore baselines alongside entailment/grounding-trained models… across three evaluation constructs…, asking whether any scorer transfers" [ding2026] Why: direct evidence that reported attribution gains may be construct-specific (C6).
- Claim: post-hoc citation verifiers disagree wildly on the same outputs depending on strictness. Quote: "On identical agent outputs the measured unsupported-citation rate ranges from about 3% to about 18% depending only on the verifier's strictness" [goo2026] Why: an 18%-vs-3% spread on identical data invalidates naive cross-paper comparison of citation metrics.
- Claim: LLM-as-judge paradigm's own survey concedes a limitations section. Quote: "we provide a detailed analysis of the limitations of LLM judges and discuss potential future directions." [li-judge-survey] Why: authoritative survey acknowledging judge unreliability (position/self-bias documented elsewhere).

### (b) Mitigations beyond basic RAG and their limits
- Claim: self-reflective/adaptive retrieval (retrieve only when needed, critique own output) is a benchmarked mitigation. Quote: "However, indiscriminately retrieving and incorporating a fixed number of retrieved passages, regardless of whether retrieval is necessary, or passages are relevant, di…" [selfrag] Why: canonical self-checking mitigation with published QA/factuality gains (ICLR 2024).
- Claim: prompt-based abstention nearly halves misleading-answer rate but a conflict check does better at coverage cost. Quote: "Models abstain reliably when evidence is missing, but under explicit abstention prompting still answer 41.6% of misleading questions, with 63% of those answers echoing the planted wrong entity verbatim… A generator-side conflict check cuts the rate to 13.3% but discards many correct answers, while an NLI verifier recovers that coverage but fails when parametric memory and the misleading passage agree" [grab-rag] Why: quantifies both gain and limit of abstention/NLI verification.
- Claim: self-consistency + conformal prediction gives a provable hallucination-rate bound. Quote: "we propose using the LLM itself to self-evaluate the similarity between each of its sampled responses… we then further leverage conformal prediction techniques to develop an abstention procedure that benefits from rigorous theoretical guarantees on the hallucination rate" [kuzborskij2024] Why: strongest formal result for self-consistency-based abstention; still a closed-book QA setting.
- Claim: citation-attribution systems should be scored on abstention as well as correctness, because RAG removes abstention. Quote: "Advanced RAG lowers HR relative to Naïve RAG (65.4% vs. 87.6%) but reduces AR from 5.0% to 0%. Under adversarial metadata, several systems exceed 85% HR" [reasons2024] Why: empirical gain (HR −22pp) with the central limit (no abstention left).

### (c) Counter-evidence / strongest criticism
- Claim: flagship commercial RAG legal tools hallucinate far more than marketed, in a preregistered study. Quote: "we find that the AI research tools made by LexisNexis (Lexis+ AI) and Thomson Reuters (Westlaw AI-Assisted Research and Ask Practical Law AI) each hallucinate between 17% and 33% of the time. We also document substantial differences between systems in responsiveness and accuracy." [magesh2024] Why: production-grade, preregistered counter-evidence to "RAG solves hallucination" (dates: 2024-05, updated 2026-08).
- Claim: vendors' own claims were the premise tested. Quote: "Lexis: '…Lexis+ AI delivers 100% hallucination-free linked legal citations…'" [magesh2024] Why: shows the marketing-vs-measurement gap the checklist calls inflated claims.
- Claim: legal RAG still hallucinates in the worst case in nearly half of answers (GDPR/French civil law, 8 systems). Quote: "In the worst case, BM25+GPT-5 hallucinates in roughly one out of five answers, whereas Llama3-8B-based systems hallucinate in nearly half of the generated answers." [das2026] Why: recent (2026-08) domain replication showing retrieval quality alone ≠ faithfulness.
- Claim: volume-based accuracy masks abstention differences; deployed RAG systems differ more in whether they answer than in correctness. Quote: "we find that accuracy when answering is closely clustered across systems (97.0–98.0%), while canary violation rates differ roughly sixfold (16.7% vs. 98.1%). The systems are separated less by what they answer correctly than by whether they answer at all when they should not" [penalty2026] Why: production-style evaluation showing benchmark design (scoring rule) inflates/distorts rankings (C8).
- Claim: even with relevant context, commercial summarization RAG hallucination rates remain non-trivial (HHEM leaderboard motivation). Quote: "Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) aims to reduce hallucinations by grounding responses in external context, yet large language models (LLMs) still frequently introduce unsupported information or contradictions even when provided with relevant context." [vectara-faithjudge] Why: industry-scale counter-evidence that grounding ≠ zero hallucination.

## Conflicts
- LLM judges: review says LLM judges are the metric "most highly correlated with human judgement" [malin2025], while the same period's survey and industry work document judge limitations and redesign them [li-judge-survey, vectara-faithjudge], and scorer audits find no scorer transfers across constructs [ding2026]. Stronger side: the critical work (multiple independent audits); the correlation claim holds only per-task.
- RAG effect direction: papers still report RAG lowering hallucination [reasons2024, selfrag], but preregistered production tests find 17–33% residual hallucination [magesh2024] and ~48% worst-case [das2026]. Both true at different baselines: RAG reduces vs. bare chatbot but falls far short of "hallucination-free".
