# SQ1: How does retrieval at inference change what an LM conditions on, why does that lower hallucination, and what quantitative evidence exists?

## In brief
- Mechanism: RAG replaces purely parametric conditioning with joint conditioning on input + top-K retrieved passages (a "non-parametric memory"), so each generated token is produced from retrieved evidence rather than only weights; this makes knowledge inspectable/updatable [lewis2020][shuster2021].
- Lewis et al. 2020 report RAG (BART + DPR over Wikipedia) generates "more specific, diverse and factual language" than a parametric-only baseline on knowledge-intensive generation [lewis2020].
- Strongest head-to-head number: Shuster et al. 2021 (WoW) — retrieval-augmented models cut hallucinated responses by >60% (human eval); hallucination 68.2% (BART-Large) → 7.9% (FiD-RAG), knowledgeability 34.1% → 96.1%; gains 70% (in-distribution) / 85% (out-of-distribution) [shuster2021].
- Residual-error benchmarks: RAGTruth (ACL 2024) — 17,790 RAG responses from 6 LLMs; 7,664 (~43%) contain hallucinations; QA span density per 100 words: GPT-4 0.06 vs Llama-2-7B 0.59; hallucination is more often "baseless" than "conflicting" [ragtruth24][ragtruth-benchlm][ragtruth-repo].
- HaluEval (EMNLP 2023) — ChatGPT fabricates unverifiable info in ~19.5% of user queries; external knowledge improves hallucination recognition [haleval2023].
- Counterpoint: AboulEla et al. 2025 (HaluEval/SQuADv2/TriviaQA subsets) — naive RAG lifts accuracy over base LLM (10.18%→44.56% on HaluEval) but "did not result in significant performance improvements" vs a fine-tuned model [abouela2025].

## Findings
1. **Mechanism — conditioning on retrieved passages.** Lewis et al.: "the retriever (Dense Passage Retriever, henceforth DPR) provides latent documents conditioned on the input, and the seq2seq model (BART) then conditions on these latent documents together with the input to generate the output" [lewis2020]. The generator is p(y_i | x, z, y_1:i-1), with RAG-Sequence (one doc for all tokens) and RAG-Token (per-token doc) marginalizing over top-K documents [lewis2021→lewis2020].
2. **Why it lowers hallucination.** Lewis et al.: parametric-only models "may produce 'hallucinations'"; hybrid models help "because knowledge can be directly revised and expanded, and accessed knowledge can be inspected and interpreted." They find "RAG models generate more specific, diverse and factual language than a state-of-the-art parametric-only seq2seq baseline" (MS-MARCO/Jeopardy generation; no single % given) [lewis2020].
3. **Quantitative study 1 — Shuster et al. 2021, Wizard of Wikipedia (human eval, BART-Large base):** "standard (non-retrieval augmented) large language models indeed suffer from hallucination, whereas our best models substantially curtail the issue, reducing hallucinated responses by over 60%" and "knowledgeability metric gains over the baseline are 70% for in distribution data and 85% for out-of-distribution data" [shuster2021]. Table 4: BART-Large Hall. 68.2 / Knowl. 34.1; RAG-Seq (5 docs) Hall. 9.6; FiD-RAG (5 docs) Hall. 7.9 / Knowl. 96.1 [shuster2021]. Out-of-distribution gain is larger, supporting the mechanism that retrieval supplements "what is simply not in the weights" [shuster2021].
4. **Quantitative study 2 — RAGTruth (ACL 2024), ~18K naturally generated RAG responses, 6 LLMs, QA + data-to-text + news summarization, word-level annotated:** "RAGTruth comprises nearly 18,000 naturally generated responses from diverse LLMs using RAG"; "the generation of information baseless in the context was significantly more prevalent than the generation of information conflicting with the context"; "GPT-4-0613 exhibited the lowest hallucination frequency"; "Mistral-7B-Instruct model generated the highest number of responses containing hallucinations" [ragtruth24]. Numbers (transcribed from the ACL tables): QA hallucinating responses GPT-4 48, GPT-3.5 75, Llama-2-7B 510, Mistral-7B 378; density/spans per 100 words QA: GPT-4 0.06, GPT-3.5 0.12, Llama-2-7B 0.59, Mistral 0.59 [ragtruth-benchlm]. Overall 7,664 of 17,790 responses hallucinate (≈43.1%), 14,289 spans [ragtruth-repo]. A detection-then-selection pipeline cut response-level hallucination rate 63.2% relatively (Llama/Mistral pair, 51.8→19.3%) and 51.0% (GPT pair, 10.9→4.8%) [ragtruth-benchlm].
5. **Benchmark context — HaluEval (EMNLP 2023), 35K samples across QA/dialogue/summarization + 5K user queries:** "ChatGPT is likely to generate hallucinated content in specific topics by fabricating unverifiable information (i.e., about 19.5% user queries)" and "the hallucination recognition can be improved by providing external knowledge or adding reasoning steps" [haleval2023].
6. **Follow-up mechanism — Self-RAG (arXiv 2023):** "Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), an ad hoc approach that augments LMs with retrieval of relevant knowledge, decreases such issues. However, indiscriminately retrieving and incorporating a fixed number of retrieved passages, regardless of whether retrieval is necessary, or passages are relevant, [is suboptimal]" — motivates adaptive/reflective retrieval as follow-up to Lewis et al. [selfrag2023].
7. **Direct base-vs-RAG comparison — AboulEla et al. 2025 (IEEE SysCon, HaluEval/SQuADv2/TriviaQA subsets, 2025-04-07):** "the base model achieved accuracies of 10.18%, 12.67%, and 5.46% respectively; Naive RAG resulted in 44.56%, 19.04%, and 35.32% accuracies; while the fine-tuned LLM model's accuracies were 72.5%, 72.31%, and 88.7% respectively" [abouela2025].

## Conflicts
- **Does RAG reliably reduce hallucination?** Shuster et al. (68.2%→7.9%, >60% reduction) and AboulEla et al. (10.18%→44.56% accuracy on HaluEval subset) both show RAG beats the no-retrieval baseline, but AboulEla et al. conclude "incorporating RAG solutions did not result in significant performance improvements" relative to a fine-tuned LLM, and "RAG topologies might not consistently enhance LLM reliability in practical situations" [abouela2025 vs shuster2021].
- **Magnitude varies by setting:** 19.5% (HaluEval, ChatGPT, no RAG) [haleval2023] vs ~43% of RAG responses (RAGTruth) [ragtruth-repo] vs 68.2% (WoW, BART-Large, 2021 model) [shuster2021] — different models/tasks/eras; not directly comparable.
- **Within Shuster et al.:** retrieval-fusion variants (RAG-Token) were "prone to knowledge hallucination more readily" than non-fusion RAG (Hall. 17.0–21.5 vs 7.9–9.6) — more retrieval fusion ≠ less hallucination [shuster2021].

## Gaps
- No single study measures RAG-vs-no-RAG hallucination on a modern LLM with one unified metric; Shuster 2021 uses 2021 models, RAGTruth has no no-RAG baseline (it measures RAG residual error, not reduction).
- Could not retrieve specific FActScore/TruthfulQA numbers from the Self-RAG paper body (abstract is qualitative only); the pinnaclepubs comparative table (Self-RAG FActScore 81.2% vs 58.4% baseline) lacks authors/date and was excluded as weak.
- The ~43.1% figure is computed from the official repo's table (7,664/17,790); the paper's prose does not state the aggregate percentage in the sections I fetched.
