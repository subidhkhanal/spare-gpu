# SQ2: Taxonomies/frameworks classifying RAG hallucination by origin

## In brief
- Named origin-based frameworks found: Barnett et al.'s "Seven Failure Points" (2024) stages the failure along the RAG pipeline — missing content, missed top docs, not in context, not extracted, wrong format, incorrect specificity, incomplete [barnett2024]. Zhang & Zhang's review (2025) groups RAG hallucination into exactly two origin stages: retrieval failure and generation deficiency, with four and five sub-causes respectively [zhang2025].
- RAGTruth (ACL 2024) offers the leading claim-level taxonomy for RAG: four types — Evident Conflict, Subtle Conflict, Evident Introduction of Baseless Information, Subtle Introduction of Baseless Information — plus auxiliary labels implicit_true and due_to_null [ragtruth2024]. Baseless information (unsupported-by-context) was clearly more common than conflict (contradicting-context), especially in QA [ragtruth2024].
- RAGEC (EACL 2026) groups 16 error types by pipeline stage (chunking/retrieval/reranking/generation) and reports frequencies: on DragonBall-EN chunking 29.7%, retrieval 31.5%, reranking 7.1%, generation 32.7%; "Fabricated Content" (plain hallucination) was "relatively rare" [ragec2026]. Its generation block directly matches the checklist concepts: Fabricated Content (answer unsupported by context), Parametric Overreliance, Misinterpretation (context misused), Contextual Misalignment [ragec2026].
- Zhao et al.'s attribution survey (2026) maps six RAG hallucination types to retriever vs. generator origin: Overconfidence, Outdatedness, Unverifiability, Instruction Deviation, Context Inconsistency, Reasoning Deficiency [zhao2026].
- Garani et al. (TrustNLP 2026) subsume prior work into 33 failure modes over 7 pipeline stages and note retrieval and generation failures are the most studied [garani2026].

## Findings

**F1 — Barnett et al. Seven Failure Points (pipeline-origin framework).** Quote: "1. FP1 Missing Content The first fail case is when asking a question that cannot be answered from the available documents... 2. FP2 Missed the Top Ranked Documents... 3. FP3 Not in Context - Consolidation strategy Limitations... 4. FP4 Not Extracted Here the answer is present in the context, but the large language model failed to extract out the correct answer." [barnett2024] Why it matters: this is the earliest widely-cited named framework that places each failure at a pipeline stage (retrieval vs. generation), matching "retrieval failure" and "context not used" categories. No frequencies reported.

**F2 — Two-stage origin split: retrieval failure vs. generation deficiency (Zhang & Zhang review, Mathematics 2025).** Quote: "hallucinations in RAG models arise from two primary stages: retrieval failure and generation deficiency... In the retrieval stage, the retrieval module may not be able to provide accurate contextual information due to the unreliability of the data source, the ambiguity of the user query, or the limitations of the retriever and retrieval strategy. In the generation phase, the generation module may generate content inconsistent with the retrieved information or input requirements due to context noise, context conflict, middle curse or alignment problems, or inaccurate generation due to limited model capabilities." [zhang2025] Why it matters: this is the cleanest survey-level two-way origin taxonomy matching the sub-question's framing. Sub-causes: retrieval = data source, query, retriever, retrieval strategy problems; generation = context noise, context conflict, middle curse, alignment problem, ability boundaries.

**F3 — RAGTruth four-type claim taxonomy with severity.** Quote: "we categorize the hallucination in the RAG setting into four types... Evident Conflict: for when generative content presents direct contraction or opposition to the provided information... Subtle Conflict... altering the intended contextual meaning... Evident Introduction of Baseless Information: for when generated content includes information not substantiated in the provided information... Subtle Introduction of Baseless Information: is when generated content extends beyond the provided information by incorporating inferred details, insights, or sentiments." [ragtruth2024] Why it matters: the dominant benchmark taxonomy; its two axes (conflict vs. baseless = intrinsic-like vs. extrinsic-like; evident vs. subtle = severity) cover "context misused" and "answer unsupported by context". Auxiliary labels: implicit_true ("this span is correct while the info is not mentioned in the context") and due_to_null (null JSON field read as negation) [ragtruth-repo].

**F4 — RAGTruth relative frequencies.** Quote: "the generation of information baseless in the context was significantly more prevalent than the generation of information conflicting with the context, especially for the question answering tasks. Within the two major categories of baseless info and conflict, the more severe hallucinations, namely Evident baseless info and Evident conflict, respectively, account for a significant portion." [ragtruth2024] Also: "Across the three tasks, the date-to-text writing task exhibited the highest frequency of hallucinations"; GPT-4-0613 had the lowest hallucination frequency; "About 17.7% hallucination spans in the data-to-text writing tasks are related to null values in the JSON data." [ragtruth2024] Why it matters: the only well-powered relative-frequency result among the taxonomies — baseless/unsupported claims dominate over contradictions. Exact percentages per type are in Figure 2 (not extractable as text); annotator agreement 91.8% response-level, 78.8% span-level [ragtruth2024].

**F5 — RAGEC pipeline-stage taxonomy + frequencies (EACL 2026).** Quote: "Errors are grouped by the pipeline stage that caused them: chunking, retrieval, reranking, or generation." Generation-stage names: "Abstention Failure... Fabricated Content: The response includes information not present in the source document chunks and is unverifiable... Parametric Overreliance: The response depends on the LLM's internal knowledge rather than the source document chunks... Misinterpretation: The generator misuses or misrepresents the source document chunks... Contextual Misalignment: The response is correct but does not address the query..." [ragec2026] Why it matters: it explicitly encodes context-not-used (Parametric Overreliance, Abstention Failure), context-misused (Misinterpretation), and answer-unsupported-by-context (Fabricated Content) inside a named, code-released taxonomy.

**F6 — RAGEC frequency distribution.** Quote: "Fabricated Content (E10)—often referred to as hallucination—is relatively rare in our analysis. This observation stands in contrast to the focus on hallucination in recent research... Our findings suggest that, in the context of RAG, other types of errors such as retrieval or chunking issues are more prevalent." [ragec2026] Percentages (DragonBall-EN, modal labels, Table 2): Chunking 29.7%, Retrieval 31.5%, Reranking 7.1%, Generation 32.7%; within generation: Misinterpretation 178, Fabricated Content 34, Incomplete Answer 17, Contextual Misalignment 14 (of 832 total). [ragec2026] Why it matters: only named taxonomy found with an explicit relative-frequency table by origin.

**F7 — Zhao et al. attribution taxonomy: six types mapped to retriever/generator.** Quote: "This section presents a taxonomy of hallucination types specific to RAG systems, based on the interaction between the retriever and generator... Overconfidence... Outdatedness... Unverifiability... Instruction Deviation... Context Inconsistency... Reasoning Deficiency." Table 1 marks each as retriever-origin, generator-origin or both: Outdatedness = retriever; Overconfidence, Context Inconsistency, Reasoning Deficiency = generator; Unverifiability, Instruction Deviation = both. [zhao2026] Why it matters: an explicit origin-attribution taxonomy (retriever vs. generator) tailored to RAG, per Section 3.7: "Retriever-related hallucinations arise from outdated, irrelevant, or incomplete references... generator-related hallucinations result from the model's response formulation."

**F8 — Garani et al. 33 failure modes / 7 stages.** Quote: "We present a systematic taxonomy of 33 failure modes across 7 pipeline stages — ingestion, representation, retrieval, generation, evaluation, deployment, and agentic orchestration... retrieval and generation failures are comparatively well-studied, while representation, evaluation, and agentic orchestration failures remain under-investigated." Generation failures F13–F17: "Hallucination Despite Retrieved Context... Conflicting Information Unresolved... Incomplete / Partial Answers... Incorrect Specificity... Wrong Output Format"; F13 is decomposed into "content fabrication, parameter override, and context misalignment subtypes" via Cresswell et al. [garani2026] Why it matters: the broadest recent pipeline taxonomy; grades evidence per mode (Strong/Moderate/Limited).

**F9 — Prior-survey precedent for factuality vs. faithfulness.** Quote: "We categorize hallucination into two primary types: factuality hallucination and faithfulness hallucination... faithfulness hallucination captures the divergence of generated content from user input... further subdivided into instruction inconsistency... context inconsistency... and logical inconsistency." [zhang2023-siren] Why it matters: the standard LLM-level distinction that RAG taxonomies inherit: faithfulness/context inconsistency = unsupported-by-context, which is the RAG analogue of extrinsic hallucination.

## Conflicts
- RAGTruth says baseless/unsupported content is "significantly more prevalent" than context-conflict, especially in QA [ragtruth2024]; RAGEC says plain Fabricated Content is "relatively rare" and retrieval/chunking errors dominate [ragec2026]. These are partly compatible (RAGTruth conditions on annotated hallucination spans only; RAGEC counts all error causes including chunking/retrieval on curated vs. domain corpora) but the practical emphasis differs: RAGTruth studies which hallucination types occur once hallucinating; RAGEC studies which pipeline stage causes errors overall. DragonBall-EN generation errors = 32.7% vs. CLAPnq generation = 76.35% of errors — corpus choice swings the origin distribution substantially [ragec2026].
- Granularity conflicts: two-stage (retrieval/generation) [zhang2025] vs. four-stage (chunking/retrieval/reranking/generation) [ragec2026] vs. seven-stage (ingestion→agentic) [garani2026]; no cross-walk standard exists.

## Gaps
- Exact per-type percentages in RAGTruth Figure 2 are not machine-extractable from the PDF text; only the qualitative ordering (baseless > conflict) and the 17.7% null-value figure are quoted.
- No single survey yet unifies origin-based (pipeline) taxonomies with claim-level (RAGTruth) taxonomies; mapping between RAGEC E10–E14 and RAGTruth's four types is untested.
- Intrinsic vs. extrinsic terminology is not used consistently inside RAG papers; most RAG taxonomies re-express it as conflict vs. baseless or faithfulness vs. factuality.
