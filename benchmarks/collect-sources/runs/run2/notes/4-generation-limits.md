# Generation-side limits on RAG's hallucination reduction (C5)

## In brief
- Even with correct retrieval available, generation-side failures persist: hallucinated sentences coexist with correct answers — ~25% of Falcon-180B outputs containing ground-truth answers still contain ungrounded claims [goyal2024groundedness]; on WildHallucinations retrieval "only slightly reduces hallucinations but does not eliminate hallucinations" [zhao2024wild]; retrieval still hallucinates 19.7% of answers even with textbook passages supplied [bell2026medical].
- Models do not reliably arbitrate retrieval vs. priors: GPT-4o overrides its own correct prior with perturbed retrieved text >60% of the time, while correct context fixes prior errors only ~94% and fails ~20% of the time by re-asserting the prior [wu2024clash].
- Irrelevant/distractor context measurably degrades generation: semantically-related irrelevant docs raised misrepresentation to 22.5% (GPT-3.5) / 83.5% (Llama2-7B) [xie2024irrelevant]; fixed-length multi-document sweep cost up to 20% [levy2025moredocs]; distractor-heavy contexts fell 18.61 pts (-33%) [ceur2025rethinking]; "first drop of ink" shows a small hard-distractor fraction causes most of the damage [anon2026ink].
- Long-context models don't reliably use context: U-shaped position effect, "performance degrades significantly when changing the position of relevant information… even for explicitly long-context models" [liu2024lost]; related-task studies confirm multi-document count itself hurts beyond length [levy2025moredocs].
- Faithfulness ≠ correctness: correct answers frequently come with unsupported sentences [goyal2024groundedness]; citation correctness alone is insufficient without faithfulness [wallat2024correctness]; copying passages can be scored faithful without grounding [pham2026copying].
- Abstention is miscalibrated in both directions: over-refusal — RALMs "refuse questions they could have answered correctly" when documents are irrelevant [zhou2025doknow]; under-abstention — 65–91% over-answer under conflicting evidence [zhang2026sufficiency]; misleading context answers 41.6% of the time vs. ≤3% for missing context [grabrag2026]. Refusal training can introduce 40–90% incorrect refusals on answerable items [rajapakse2026rsrr].

## Findings

1. **Correct context doesn't guarantee faithful, correct output.** "even for the largest models analyzed (Falcon 180B; Penedo et al., 2023), approximately 25% of the outputs that contain ground-truth answers are not grounded." [goyal2024groundedness] — shows faithfulness failures survive correct retrieval; correctness metrics miss them.
2. **Retrieval reduces but doesn't eliminate hallucination.** "given the same base models, adding a retrieval component only slightly reduces hallucinations but does not eliminate hallucinations." [zhao2024wild] — direct evidence that hallucination persists despite retrieval; 118,785 generations, 15 LLMs.
3. **Hallucination persists with passages supplied.** "with the passages provided, LLaMA-70B-Instruct hallucinated in 19.7% of answers (95% CI 18.6 to 20.7) even though 98.8% of prompt responses received maximal plausibility" [bell2026medical] — generation-side failure with gold evidence in context (5,543 QA pairs).
4. **Prior-vs-retrieval conflict — model ignores its own prior (context bias).** "LLMs are susceptible to adopting incorrect retrieved content, overriding their own correct prior knowledge over 60% of the time" when retrieved content is perturbed [wu2024clash] — sycophancy toward context; 1,200+ questions, six LLMs incl. GPT-4o. Also: "a slope of -0.45... 4.5% decrease in the likelihood of the LLM preferring the contextual information for every 10% increase in the probability of the model's prior response" — adherence inversely tracks prior confidence.
5. **Prior-vs-retrieval conflict — model ignores retrieval when priors are strong.** "providing the correct retrieved information fixes most model mistakes (94% accuracy)... in the minority of cases where providing the retrieved content fails to correct the LLM, we find that the model simply responds with its original prior answer about 20% of the time." [wu2024clash] — retrieval ignored despite correctness; ~6% of corrected cases still fail.
6. **Dose-response for context override.** "a single false-premise sentence flips the answer to the falsehood on 18% (4/22, 1.5B) and 26% (5/19, 0.5B) of them... an emphatic, repeated premise... raises the override rate from 18% to 77% on the 1.5B and from 26% to 100% on the 0.5B." [ctxconflict2026] — controlled false-context experiment; more insistent context wins regardless of truth.
7. **Irrelevant-but-related context hijacks answers.** "compared to 'Unrelated Information', there is a notable increase in the misrepresentation ratio for 'Partially Related Information' and 'Related Information'" — GPT-3.5 Turbo MR 5.5%→22.5%, Llama2-7B 72.2%→83.5% (PopQA); at 3:1 irrelevant:relevant, Llama2-7B MR 42.4% [xie2024irrelevant] — controlled irrelevant-context study.
8. **More documents at fixed length reduce performance.** "We keep the context length and position of relevant information constant while varying the number of documents, and find that increasing the document count in RAG settings poses significant challenges for most LLMs, reducing performance by up to 20%." [levy2025moredocs] — isolates document count from length; Qwen2.5 was the exception.
9. **Distractors degrade accuracy ~33% in oracle setup.** "LLM accuracy declines as the number of distracting documents in the context increases, with a decrease of 18.61 points (-33%) when the context includes 18 distracting documents." [ceur2025rethinking] — adding context hurts even when the gold doc is present.
10. **Nonlinear distractor damage.** "as the proportion of hard distractors increases, performance drops sharply within the first small fraction, while the remainder of the range yields only marginal additional decline... filtering gains mainly come from context-length reduction rather than distractor removal" [anon2026ink] — a little wrong context does most of the damage; post-hoc filtering barely helps.
11. **Long-context models don't reliably use position/long context.** "performance can degrade significantly when changing the position of relevant information, indicating that current language models do not robustly make use of information in long input contexts... significantly degrades when models must access relevant information in the middle of long contexts, even for explicitly long-context models." [liu2024lost] — foundational context-usage failure (multi-doc QA, key-value retrieval).
12. **Retrieval can reduce accuracy; fine-tuning on mixed contexts fixes it.** "recent work has shown that retrieval augmentation can sometimes have a negative effect on performance... on five open-domain question answering benchmarks, characterizing cases when retrieval reduces accuracy... even 1,000 examples suffice to train the model to be robust to irrelevant contexts" [yoran2024robust] — retrieval itself hurts sometimes; fixable at generation side.
13. **Over-refusal with irrelevant retrieval.** "Contrary to expectations, when all retrieved documents are irrelevant, RALMs still tend to refuse questions they could have answered correctly." [zhou2025doknow] — over-abstention documented (AAAI 2025).
14. **Misleading vs. missing evidence — abstention collapses.** "answer rates stay at or below 3% when the answer is missing, yet climb to between 13.6% and 74.3% when the retrieved context includes a fluent passage supporting a wrong answer. When models do answer under misleading context, 63% of their responses echo the planted fake entity, and only 4% recover the correct answer." [grabrag2026] — controlled misleading-context study, 30 model×dataset×policy cells.
15. **Conflicting evidence → over-answering.** "Under L5 conflicting evidence, all evaluated models show high over-answer rates, ranging from 65% to 91%." [zhang2026sufficiency] — seven LLMs, five families; abstention unreliable.
16. **Refusal training can cause over-abstention.** "PPO - No Rank suffers from severe over-refusal, particularly on PubMedQA and StrategyQA, where it abstains in 40–90% of answerable cases." [rajapakse2026rsrr] — trading hallucination for over-refusal; baseline minimizes refusal errors but is distractor-sensitive.
17. **Faithfulness vs. correctness are distinct.** "citation correctness alone is insufficient. To establish trust in attributed answers, we must examine both citation correctness and citation faithfulness." [wallat2024correctness] — attribution evaluation must separate the two axes.
18. **Copying isn't grounding — faithfulness rewards can be hacked.** "A sentence copied from the passages is supported almost by construction, so trained models learn to copy rather than ground what they write." [pham2026copying] — faithfulness metric gaming; generation-side limitation of reward-based fixes.

## Conflicts
- Direction of parametric-vs-context reliance: [wu2024clash] shows >60% context bias (adopting wrong context over correct priors), whereas [goyal2024groundedness]-adjacent and [chen2022conflicts] report models mostly rely on retrieved evidence in best settings yet "prefer the answer that matches their parametric knowledge" when passages conflict with each other; [kortukov2024real] found parametric answers retained in only 0.4–6.2% of cases with real conflicting documents — much lower than earlier over-reliance reports. Stronger/more controlled studies ([wu2024clash], [xie2024irrelevant]) suggest model size, prior confidence, and context plausibility moderate the direction; no single fixed susceptibility number.
- Effect of retrieval on hallucination: [zhao2024wild] "only slightly reduces"; [bell2026medical] 19.7% persists with passages; older work reports larger reductions. Stronger large-scale benchmarks (WildHallucinations, 15 LLMs) take the position that retrieval helps but is far from sufficient.
- Under- vs. over-abstention: [grabrag2026] documents under-abstention under misleading context; [zhou2025doknow] and [rajapakse2026rsrr] document over-refusal with irrelevant evidence or refusal tuning. These co-exist: miscalibration is bidirectional depending on evidence type.

## Gaps
- No single study measures sycophancy toward retrieved context per se (agreeing with retrieved statements while knowing better) as a isolated variable — closest are context-bias ([wu2024clash]) and false-premise override ([ctxconflict2026]); user-directed sycophancy papers were out of the RAG context-behaviour frame.
- Could not retrieve full-text controlled experiment specifically isolating "adding more context does not reduce hallucination rate" (vs. accuracy); [ceur2025rethinking] and [levy2025moredocs] measure accuracy, not hallucination rate.
- Age of models: several key studies use 2023–24 models (GPT-3.5/4, Llama-2); few Nov-2026-era frontier replication numbers were found within search limits.
- [anon2026ink] and [ctxconflict2026] are non-peer-reviewed preprints (author metadata incomplete) — findings directional only.
