# Survey literature on combining RAG with other hallucination-reduction techniques, and open problems for RAG-based mitigation

## In brief
- Surveys converge: no single technique eliminates hallucination; the consensus direction is combining complementary families (retrieval + reasoning/self-consistency + post-hoc verification + model-centric training/fine-tuning) [alansari2026][li2025capability].
- Named complementary pairings: RAG + CoT/self-consistency/verification as "hybrid approaches" [alansari2026]; RAG + reasoning as two orthogonal capability axes (knowledge vs logic) whose combination targets "composite hallucinations" [li2025capability]; attribution/citation verification as a post-generation stage T4 orthogonal to retrieval [zhao2026].
- Stated trade-offs: retrieval adds latency/complexity and is bounded by source quality; reasoning/self-consistency add compute and can amplify flawed chains; decoding and fine-tuning are domain-sensitive [alansari2026].
- RAG is treated as both remedy and new error source (retriever–generator interaction) [zhao2026], and Huang et al. dedicate an open-questions item to "limitations faced by retrieval-augmented LLMs" [huang2023survey].
- Recurring open problems: standardised evaluation and LLM-judge bias, multi-turn/long-context, low-resource/multilingual, efficiency of verification loops, reliability of self-checks, knowledge boundaries [alansari2026][zhao2026][huang2023survey].

## Findings
1. Gap framing and the RAG+reasoning combination. "Existing studies often analyze different technical paradigms independently, lacking a unified perspective to understand the underlying mechanisms of different approaches and their correspondence with different types of hallucinations." And on the combination: "the combination of these two approaches holds great potential for mitigating all types of hallucinations, offering broad prospects for application." [li2025capability] Why: frames retrieval (knowledge-based) and reasoning (logic-based) as complementary axes; agentic systems integrating both target "composite hallucinations."
2. Realistic goal. "It is widely accepted that mitigation, not complete removal, is the realistic goal" (citing work that hallucination is inherent to generative models). [li2025capability] Why: sets the success bar for any combination claim.
3. Explicit combination statement (which combinations help). "Based on our analysis, we show that no single approach completely mitigates hallucination. Consequently, more effective mitigation is a combination of complementary techniques. The most promising are hybrid approaches that combine prompting or reasoning-based techniques with retrieval-based and model-centric training and adaptation strategies." [alansari2026] Why: the clearest survey-level answer to the sub-question.
4. Complementary coverage across families (detection side). "Retrieval-based detection approaches effectively deal with factual hallucinations but are extremely sensitive to the quality of external knowledge... self-consistency detection approaches can detect logical and contextual inconsistencies without relying on external evidence. However, these approaches struggle with subtle factual errors... the combination of complementary approaches (e.g., learning with uncertainty or retrieval with learning) is a promising direction to improve the overall detection robustness and accuracy." [alansari2026] Why: states which technique covers which failure mode — retrieval and self-consistency are near-orthogonal in coverage.
5. Costs and limits of combining. "Integrating retrieval results with LLMs often introduces additional latency and complexity into the generation pipeline... The computational cost of deploying reasoning-based methods can be substantial, since these methods depend on generating multiple reasoning chains... iterative and self-correction strategies cannot fully guarantee error removal, even when supported by self-verification and multi-turn feedback... if initial reasoning chains are flawed, subsequent refinements may amplify rather than correct errors." [alansari2026] Why: the main trade-off list (latency, compute, error amplification) when stacking techniques.
6. Task/domain fit of each family. "Retrieval-based approaches tend to be more beneficial in knowledge-intensive domains, such as medicine, law, and finance... reasoning-based strategies are more helpful for multi-step reasoning tasks... Prompt-based and decoding-based mitigation techniques often show gains in structured or well-defined tasks... their effectiveness declines in open-ended generation settings... model-centric approaches... struggle to generalize to unseen domains." Concluding: "no single approach can address all hallucination failure modes, underscoring the need for domain-aware, task-adaptive pipelines that combine complementary techniques." [alansari2026] Why: practical guidance on which combinations suit which deployment.
7. Status of constrained/contrastive decoding. "Decoding strategies, such as contrastive, source-aware, and DoLa methods, have improved contextual faithfulness but can still struggle with generalization across domains and sensitivity to parameter tuning." [alansari2026] Why: the only surveyed statement found on decoding's effect and limits; it is treated as a model-centric family, not RAG-specific.
8. RAG as error source; verification as separate stage. "RAG frameworks enhance LLM responses by incorporating external references but also introduce new forms of hallucination due to complex interactions between the retriever and generator." The survey's unified pipeline has four architecture-free modules: "Query Refining (T1), Reference Identification (T2), Prompt Engineering (T3), and Response Correction (T4)" — citation/attribution checks (RARR, Self-Refine, CoVe, NLI verifiers) sit in T4, i.e., post-hoc and orthogonal to retrieval. [zhao2026] Why: positions citation verification as a distinct, combinable layer on top of RAG.
9. Open problems specific to attribution/verification. "Single-Source Validation... may be insufficient for open-domain or multi-perspective questions... Reliance on External Databases and Safety Concerns... Use of LLMs as Retrieval Judges... introduces circularity: using the same class of models, which are prone to hallucination, as evaluators... Error Accumulation in CoT Reasoning... an incorrect early step can mislead the subsequent reasoning path." [zhao2026] Why: named open problems for reliability of the verification half of the stack.
10. Retrieval-augmented LLMs as an open-questions item. "We delve into the current limitations faced by retrieval-augmented LLMs in combating hallucinations, offering insights for developing more robust IR systems. Finally, we highlight the promising research directions on LLM hallucinations, including hallucination in large vision-language models and understanding of knowledge boundaries in LLM hallucinations." [huang2023survey] Why: the most-cited LLM-hallucination survey explicitly lists RAG limitations and knowledge boundaries as an agenda.
11. Evaluation and efficiency as open problems. "Self-consistency checks, uncertainty estimation, and retrieval-augmented verification, often require multiple inference iterations or access to external databases, thereby escalating computational expenses." On benchmarks: "Binary or Coarse-Grained Labels... ignore partial hallucinations and varying error severity"; on judges: "LLM-as-a-judge can be influenced by visually appealing formatting... exhibits positional effects... It is recommended to avoid using the same model to both generate and judge outputs." [alansari2026] Why: the recurring open-problem list (evaluation, efficiency) across surveys.

## Conflicts
- How far RAG alone gets: li2025capability calls RAG "the most effective and widely studied framework" for knowledge-based hallucinations and praises RAG+reasoning synergy, while huang2023survey singles out "limitations faced by retrieval-augmented LLMs" as an open question and zhao2026 argues RAG "introduces new forms of hallucination." All agree RAG helps; they differ on how close it is to a sufficient remedy. Stronger (newer, more RAG-specific) sources lean toward "helpful but incomplete."
- Self-correction/self-verification: zhao2026 presents post-generation correction (Self-Refine, CoVe, RARR) as an established effective module, whereas alansari2026 warns self-correction "cannot fully guarantee error removal" and can amplify flawed reasoning. No surveyed head-to-head resolves this.

## Gaps
- No surveyed quantitative ablation of constrained/contrastive decoding + RAG interaction was found; the combination is asserted, not measured, in the sources kept.
- Multi-hop/multi-source RAG appears only incidentally (long-context limits in zhao2026), not as a surveyed open-problem item in the fetched sections.
- A within-scope survey directly comparing RAG vs fine-tuning for faithfulness (e.g., medical systematic reviews) surfaced in search but was not fetched in full.
