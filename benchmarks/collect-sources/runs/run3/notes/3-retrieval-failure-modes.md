# What are the failure modes of the retrieval component in RAG systems, and how do they produce hallucinated or unfaithful outputs?

## In brief
- RGB benchmark (Chen et al., AAAI 2024) defines four retrieval-side failure testbeds — noisy passages, no answerable evidence (negative rejection), multi-doc integration, and counterfactual/false retrieved documents — and finds LLMs "struggle significantly in terms of negative rejection, information integration, and dealing with false information"; accuracy falls from 60% (no noise) to 43% (noise added) in English. [rgb-bench]
- Long/distracting context: "lost in the middle" — GPT-3.5-Turbo multi-document QA drops >20 points when the answer document sits mid-context; in the worst case performance is below closed-book (56.1%), i.e. retrieved context made the model worse than no context. [liu2024lost]
- Missing evidence + retrieval failures make RAG fail catastrophically: on RGB's all-negative (worst-case) setting "the performance gap between RAG and No RAG exceeds 50 points," and real-world retrieval precision is low (~70% of passages lack the true answer), with knowledge conflicts in 19.2% of cases. [astute]
- Knowledge conflicts (parametric vs retrieved) are common and hard: models identify that a conflict exists but "struggle to determine the specific conflicting knowledge and produce a response with distinct answers amidst conflicting information." [wang2023kconflict]
- Diagnostics exist: context-compliance tracing (CDD) shows misleading retrieval can drive standard RAG to 15.0% accuracy under misconception injection, and frames conflict handling as an "observability problem." [cdd2026]
- Disagreement: RGB says models show "a certain degree of noise robustness" while Astute RAG shows RAG can be far worse than no-RAG when retrieval is bad — severity depends on whether noise is partial or total. [rgb-bench] [astute]

## Findings

**Documented failure categories (benchmark evidence).** RGB "systematically investigate[s] the impact of Retrieval-Augmented Generation on different large language models... 4 fundamental abilities... noise robustness, negative rejection, information integration, and counterfactual robustness." Its testbeds operationalize: noise = "documents which are relevant to the question but do not contain any information of the answer"; negative rejection = answering "when none of the contexts provide useful information" (i.e., no relevant passage retrieved); counterfactual = "instances that can be answered directly by the LLMs, but the external documents contain factual errors." [rgb-bench] Why it matters: this is the canonical, peer-reviewed (AAAI) taxonomy of retrieval-component failure modes feeding unfaithful output (failure to reject → "misleading content").

**Measurable effect of noisy retrieval.** "Even without noise, the highest accuracy of LLMs can only reach 60% and 67% for English and Chinese, respectively. After adding noise, the highest accuracy decreases to 43% and 55%." "Performance decline becomes significant when the noise ratio is 0.4." [rgb-bench] Why it matters: quantifies accuracy loss from irrelevant-but-on-topic passages.

**No-relevant-evidence → hallucination via non-rejection.** RGB: "In real-world situations, the search engine often fails to retrieve documents containing the answers. In these cases, it is important for the model to have the capability to reject recognition and avoid generating misleading content." Evaluation: "LLMs exhibit a certain degree of noise robustness, they still struggle significantly in terms of negative rejection, information integration, and dealing with false information." [rgb-bench] Why it matters: documents that missing evidence causes unfaithful answers mainly through failure to abstain.

**Long/distracting context (lost-in-the-middle).** "GPT-3.5-Turbo's multi-document QA performance can drop by more than 20%—in the worst case, performance in 20- and 30-document settings is lower than performance without any input documents (i.e., closed-book performance; 56.1%)." The curve is U-shaped (primacy/recency bias). [liu2024lost] Why it matters: even when the relevant passage IS retrieved, its position among distractors determines whether it's used — a post-retrieval/ranker-ordering failure that yields wrong answers. Table 1: GPT-3.5-Turbo closed-book 56.1%, oracle 88.3% — the ceiling of adding evidence. [liu2024lost]

**Mechanism of distraction (attention bias).** "LLMs exhibit an U-shaped attention bias where the tokens at the beginning and at the end of its input receive higher attention, regardless of their relevance"; models "do attend to relevant contexts even when placed in the middle, but are eventually distracted by leading/ending contexts." [hsieh2024found] Why it matters: gives a mechanistic explanation for why noisy/long context degrades faithfulness; their calibration improves RAG "by up to 15 percentage points." [hsieh2024found]

**Imperfect retrieval is common and can be worse than nothing.** "roughly 70% retrieved passages do not directly contain true answers"; "19.2% of the overall data exhibit knowledge conflicts" (Claude 3.5 Sonnet); "The performance gap between RAG and No RAG exceeds 50 points" in RGB's all-negative worst case. [astute] Why it matters: directly links retrieval precision to downstream hallucination/accuracy and quantifies worst-case harm.

**Parametric-vs-retrieved conflict prevalence and asymmetry.** In conflicts, "the internal knowledge is correct on 47.4% of them, while the external knowledge is correct on the remaining 52.6%" — neither source is reliable, so blindly trusting retrieval or parameters each fails; "LLMs often select knowledge based on unreliable shortcuts." [astute] Why it matters: conflicting/outdated evidence forces a choice, and naive context-compliance makes the model repeat false retrieved claims.

**Contradictory evidence handling.** "while LLMs perform well in identifying the existence of knowledge conflicts, they struggle to determine the specific conflicting knowledge and produce a response with distinct answers amidst conflicting information." Causes cited: "misinformation, varying perspectives, time-sensitive information, or knowledge updates." [wang2023kconflict] Why it matters: models can't reliably surface contradictions → they silently blend or pick one side, producing unfaithful answers.

**Diagnostics.** CDD (Context-Driven Decomposition) "elicits contextual and prior answers, isolates the conflicting premise, and records a resolution trace that can be perturbed"; under misleading retrieval "Standard RAG reaches only 15.0%" accuracy (TruthfulQA misconception injection); frames RAG conflict handling "as an observability problem." [cdd2026] Why it matters: a concrete proposed diagnostic that separates context-compliance from parametric reliance, which plain accuracy hides.

## Conflicts
- RGB concludes LLMs show "a certain degree of noise robustness" (partial noise tolerable) [rgb-bench]; Astute RAG finds retrieval can be so bad that RAG underperforms no-RAG by >50 points [astute]. Stronger/more specific: Astute's result holds under *total* (all-negative) retrieval failure, RGB's "robustness" under partial noise — no true contradiction, severity depends on noise level. RGB also notes decline only becomes significant at noise ratio 0.4 for simple problems.
- Lost-in-the-middle shows extended-context models are "not necessarily better than their non-extended counterparts at using their input context" [liu2024lost] — against the assumption that bigger context windows fix retrieval-order problems.

## Gaps
- No single paper gives a full causal chain from a specific *ranker* error (e.g., Recall@k miss) to a measured hallucination rate; ranker-level metrics are mostly assumed (RGB notes its data "measure[s] the generator's evidence use, not the retriever ranking" per third-party summary; primary source states it evaluates LLM abilities).
- Effects on long-form *faithfulness* (entailment-style) rather than exact-match accuracy are under-measured; most numbers above are string-match accuracy.
- Outdated-evidence staleness specifically (vs. counterfactual/false docs) not separately quantified in the sources found.
- Diagnosing RAG Failures taxonomy (SSRN 7439478) abstract is truncated — full five-mode list not retrieved (Retrieval Blindness, Retrieval No...).
