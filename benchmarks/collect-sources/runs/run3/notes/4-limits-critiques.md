# Limits of RAG for hallucination — named negative results and strongest critiques

## In brief
- RAG helps single-hop factual extraction with in-scope context, but every named limit area has quantified failures: multi-hop queries (RAG systems "inadequate" [multihop-rag]; conflict + multi-hop [magic]), long-form citation quality (best models lack complete citation support 50% of the time [alce]; long-form factuality ~58% even for ChatGPT [factscore]), multi-document integration (performance degrades when evidence sits mid-context [lost-in-middle]).
- Conflicting parametric knowledge: under a worst-case misconception-injection probe "Standard RAG reaches only 15.0%" [context-compliance]; RAG chatbots "can still be misled when prompts directly contradict the model's pre-trained understanding" [ragged-edges].
- Strongest counter-evidence that RAG shifts rather than removes hallucination: commercial RAG systems and a no-retrieval baseline cluster at 97.0–98.0% accuracy when answering SimpleQA-Verified questions, while knowledge-gap canary violation rates differ sixfold (16.7% vs. 98.1%) — systems differ by whether they abstain, not by truthfulness [canary].
- Strongest published critique of the framing: an ICML 2025 position paper argues RAG/citation grounding that is literally accurate "can still be highly misleading" [position-medical].
- Retrieval does not consistently help: gains concentrate on infrequent entity-relation pairs; larger LMs already recall popular facts [retrieval-helps-or-hurts].

## Findings
1. Multi-hop is RAG's weak spot. MultiHop-RAG (Tang & Yang 2024, first multi-hop RAG benchmark): "we find that existing RAG systems are inadequate in answering multi-hop queries, which require retrieving and reasoning over multiple pieces of supporting evidence"; both experiments reveal "existing RAG methods perform unsatisfactorily in retrieving and answering multi-hop queries" [multihop-rag]. Failure appears on both retrieval and generation sides.
2. Multi-hop plus conflicting contexts. MAGIC (EMNLP 2025 Findings): "both open-source and proprietary models struggle with conflict detection—especially when multi-hop reasoning is required—and often fail to pinpoint the exact source of contradictions" [magic]. Peer-reviewed evidence that cross-document integration, not retrieval itself, is a failure point for all model families tested.
3. Long-form citation quality. ALCE (Gao et al., EMNLP 2023): "on the ELI5 dataset, even the best models lack complete citation support 50% of the time" [alce]. Puts a quantified ceiling on RAG's verifiability promise in long-form answers.
4. Long-form factuality. FActScore (Min et al., EMNLP 2023) scores the percentage of atomic facts supported by a knowledge source across 13 LMs, including "the retrieval-augmented PerplexityAI"; human-anchored result: "ChatGPT only achieves 58%" [factscore]. Even frontier and RAG-based systems get roughly half of long-form atomic facts right.
5. Multi-document integration. Lost in the Middle (Liu et al., TACL 2023): "performance can degrade significantly when changing the position of relevant information... significantly degrades when models must access relevant information in the middle of long contexts, even for explicitly long-context models" [lost-in-middle]. Names the mechanism by which retrieved evidence is simply not used: it is retrieved, then ignored by position.
6. Conflicting parametric knowledge. "Does RAG Know When Retrieval Is Wrong?" (Chen et al. 2026): "misleading retrieval can severely degrade accuracy: under a worst-case TruthfulQA misconception-injection probe, Standard RAG reaches only 15.0%" [context-compliance]. When retrieval is wrong, RAG converts retrieval errors into wrong answers; the model neither knows retrieval is wrong nor reliably keeps its prior.
7. RAG shifts the failure. "Why RAGs Hallucinate" (Do Rosario et al. 2026), penalty-aware evaluation of three commercial RAG systems plus a no-retrieval baseline on SimpleQA-Verified: "accuracy when answering is closely clustered across systems (97.0–98.0%), while canary violation rates differ roughly sixfold (16.7% vs. 98.1%)" [canary]. Strongest quantitative case that RAG does not remove hallucination but relocates it to ungrounded parametric guessing when the knowledge base has no answer.
8. Critique of the framing. ICML 2025 position paper (Wong, Ali et al. 2025): "even when these methods produce literally accurate content drawn from source documents sans hallucinations, they can still be highly misleading"; models "decontextualize facts, omit critical relevant sources, and reinforce patient misconceptions or biases" [position-medical]. Argues the accuracy/truthfulness yardstick RAG optimizes misses an entire class of harmful output.
9. Most favorable empirical anchor. RAGged Edges (Feldman et al. 2024): with retrieved context responses were judged accurate "approximately 94% of the time" vs. 7.31% without, yet "RAG increases accuracy in some cases, but can still be misled when prompts directly contradict the model's pre-trained understanding" [ragged-edges]. Shows the gain can be ~13x in-scope while conceding the contradiction failure.
10. Retrieval is not uniformly beneficial. NaACL 2024 (Maekawa et al., WiTQA): "Our extensive experiments with diverse LMs and retrievers reveal when retrieval does not consistently enhance LMs from the viewpoints of fact-centric popularity"; "larger LMs excel in recalling popular facts" [retrieval-helps-or-hurts]. RAG's marginal value concentrates on infrequent entity-relation pairs; for popular facts parametric memory already suffices.

## Conflicts
- Magnitude of benefit: [ragged-edges] reports ~13x accuracy improvement with in-scope context (94% vs. 7.31%) — the optimistic anchor. [canary] finds near-identical when-answering accuracy (97.0–98.0%) across RAG and no-retrieval systems on SimpleQA-Verified, with differentiation only in abstention; [retrieval-helps-or-hurts] finds retrieval does not consistently enhance LMs. Reconciliation: gains are real but conditional (single-hop, in-scope, evidence present) rather than a general hallucination fix; different task distributions (hallucination-inducing prompts vs. factoid QA) likely drive the gap.
- Direction of conflict failures: [magic], [context-compliance], and [ragged-edges] all agree models fail under conflicting evidence; no kept source claims RAG reliably resolves conflicts with parametric knowledge.

## Gaps
- The classic "RAG vs. fine-tuning" comparison (Li et al.) could not be verified — a guessed arXiv ID was wrong and the search budget was exhausted.
- FRAMES faithfulness numbers (Jiang et al. 2024) and long-form RAG-specific benchmarks (e.g., LongRAG) were not fetched within budget.
- "In Defense of RAG in the Era of Evolving LLMs" (Yoran et al. 2024), the main published rebuttal defending RAG, could not be located within the 8-search budget; the optimistic side is represented by [ragged-edges] and the conditional-gains finding in [retrieval-helps-or-hurts].
- [canary] and [context-compliance] are 2026 preprints; their numbers are not yet peer-reviewed.
- No single kept benchmark isolates RAG's marginal effect on hallucination rates with a clean RAG-vs-no-RAG design on long-form tasks; [canary] is the closest.
